RRI Recruitment 2026 : Job Overview

Raman Research Institute (RRI), in collaboration with the IITM CDoT Samgnya Technology Foundation, has invited applications for two project-based Quantum Scientist / Quantum Engineer positions in its Quantum Information and Computing (QuIC) laboratory under India's National Quantum Mission. The consolidated salary is ₹1,00,000–1,50,000 per month with a 3% increase each year, for a PhD in experimental optics or photonics. Apply only through the online form at safal2.rri.res.in by 31 December 2026; the first tenure is one year.

About the QuIC Laboratory

The QuIC laboratory at Raman Research Institute leads QuEPRAN, the National Quantum Mission technology group building a multi-node quantum repeater network for entanglement-based quantum communication. Partners are HRI Prayagraj, IIT Tirupati, IIT Patna and IISER Mohali, and Professor Urbasi Sinha is the Lead Principal Investigator. The first QuEPRAN node is being built at RRI.

The two positions are for experimental physicists and engineers with a PhD in optics. The notice lists one set of criteria for the post. Pay and Tenure

The salary is ₹1,00,000–1,50,000 per month (consolidated), with a 3% increase each year. The selection committee sets the starting salary within this range, based on the candidate's fit with the essential and desirable criteria and performance at interview. A consumables grant of up to ₹2,00,000 per year is available for the selected scientist's research. The tenure is one year in the first instance. It is extendable for the duration of the project (currently until 31 March 2031), subject to satisfactory performance in six-monthly reviews by an expert committee. The notice calls this a project-based position, and remuneration shall depend on availability of project funding.

RRI Recruitment 2026 Job Description

Lead experiments on one or more subsystems of the repeater node: colour centres in diamond (nitrogen-vacancy, silicon-vacancy and other vacancy centres), entangled-photon sources compatible with solid-state quantum memories, light–matter interfaces, and Bell-state measurement and entanglement swapping. Design, build and commission the node's optical, cryogenic and detection systems, and set the standard of calibration and documentation the laboratory works to. Supervise JRFs and PhD students day to day in experiment, analysis and writing. Take experiments through to publication, as lead author on work led and as co-author on the papers of the supervised students. Contribute to the technology group's progress reviews and reports, and to coordination with consortium partners, including travel within India where the project requires it. Selection and Interview

Applications will be screened against the essential and desirable criteria. Shortlisted candidates will be interviewed, preferably in person, and asked to give a short talk on their research and on the experiment they would lead in QuIC. Shortlisted candidates must bring copies of degree certificates and grade sheets for all degrees, school final and higher secondary examinations to the interview.
