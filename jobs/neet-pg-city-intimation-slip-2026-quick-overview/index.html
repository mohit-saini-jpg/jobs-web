What Is the NEET PG City Intimation Slip?

The NEET PG city intimation slip is a preliminary notice, not the final hall ticket. It informs a candidate which city their exam centre will be located in, so they can plan travel and accommodation ahead of exam day. It does not mention the exact exam venue address, reporting time, or seating details - that information is provided only in the admit card.

How the NEET PG 2026 Test City Is Allotted

According to the NBEMS information bulletin, test city and test centre allotment for NEET-PG 2026 is not done on a first-come-first-serve basis. Submitting the application early does not give any candidate a preferential right to a particular test state, city, or centre. Instead:

  • Candidates are first arranged in a randomized sequence by the computerized allocation system.
  • Allotment is then carried out based on the test-state preferences the candidate submitted at the time of registration.
  • Candidates typically select up to three preferred test states during registration, with their home state usually taken as the first preference.

How to Download the NEET PG City Intimation Slip 2026

Candidates can follow these steps to check and download their NEET PG 2026 city intimation slip:

  • Visit the official NBEMS website: natboard.edu.in.
  • Click on the "Examinations" section and select "NEET-PG."
  • Click on the relevant "Application" link.
  • The NEET PG 2026 session page will appear on screen.
  • Click on the "Login" link.
  • Enter your login credentials - User ID and Password (or application number and date of birth, as applicable).
  • Go to the candidate dashboard and look for the "City Intimation Slip" link.
  • Click the link to view and download your NEET PG 2026 city intimation slip. Save a copy and take a printout for reference.

NBEMS has also stated that exam city information will be sent to the registered email IDs of successfully registered candidates, in addition to being available on the login dashboard.

What Is NEET PG 2026 Used For?

NEET-PG 2026 is the single eligibility-cum-entrance examination for admission to MD/MS/PG Diploma courses for the 2026- 27 academic session, covering:

  • All India 50% quota seats across States and Union Territories
  • State quota seats across States and Union Territories
  • All private medical colleges, institutions, universities, and deemed universities
  • Armed Forces Medical Services Institutions
  • Post-MBBS DNB courses, Direct 6-year DrNB courses, and Post-MBBS NBEMS Diploma courses

Admission to INI institutions (like AIIMS New Delhi and PGIMER) for PG medical courses is done separately through INI-CET, not NEET-PG.

Total PG Medical Seats in India

According to figures shared by the government and reported in the media around the exam cycle, India has approximately 86,360 PG medical seats, with Karnataka reported to have the highest number at around 10,092 seats. This figure reflects the overall national seat count reported at the time and may not represent the exact seat matrix for the 2026-27 admission cycle - candidates should verify final seat numbers through the Medical Counselling Committee (MCC) and respective state counselling authorities closer to counselling.

Important Instructions for Candidates

  • The city intimation slip only confirms your allotted exam city - it is not valid for entry into the exam hall.
  • Keep your login credentials (User ID and Password) safe and accessible; you will need them again to download the admit card.
  • Cross-check all details on the city slip carefully; if there is a discrepancy, refer to the contact/helpdesk details provided on natboard.edu.in.
  • Do not rely on unofficial websites or social media for your city allotment - always confirm through the official NBEMS login.
  • Regularly check natboard.edu.in for updates on the admit card, exam-day guidelines, and any changes to the schedule.
  • Certain application details - such as name, mobile number, email ID, test centre, and nationality - generally cannot be changed once submitted, so verify your test-city choice carefully at the registration/correction stage in future cycles.
